## Publications

**Dust to Dust: Tracing Air Pollution’s Impact on Work Accidents** <br>
with [Ismael Moreno-Martinez](https://ismaelmorenomartinez.eu/) <br> 
[Paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/739151) <br> <i>Journal of the Association of Environmental and Resource Economists</i> 

###### Presented at: SAEe 2025, 39th AIEL Conference, 24th EAERE Annual Conference, 2nd Young AERNA Day, 10th Atlantic Workshop on Energy and Environmental Economics; Early Career Workshop on the Environment, Climate Change and Disasters, 1st International Conference of the Georgian Economic Association, Microeconometrics Working Group EUI, Norges Bank, Institute for Labour Law and Industrial Relations in the European Union

###### Coverage: [La Fonte](https://lafonte.eui.eu/2024/05/09/dust-to-dust-how-natural-air-pollution-induces-work-accidents/)

  <details>
    <summary> Abstract </summary>
    This study provides novel causal estimates of the effect of air pollution on workplace safety using data on the universe of work accidents reported in Spain (2010–19). We focus on a near-worldwide natural source of air pollution: mineral dust precipitation. The results indicate that a day of dust precipitation increases work accidents by 1.4%. Our estimates reveal that dust events’ overall burden on workplace safety are of the same order of magnitude as those of high temperatures. Impacts are widespread, spanning most worker and accident characteristics, consistent with dust pollution inducing human error across a broad range of tasks and activities. We find significant effects for workers along most of the wage distribution, though we estimate null impacts for those in the top wage quintile. Dust precipitation induces occupational injuries both mild and severe, including those requiring over two months of sick leave.
  </details>

###### Prizes: [Luis Toharia Grant](https://www.aeet.eu/en/becas-luis-toharia/) for young researchers in Labour Economics
###### Coverage: [La Fonte](https://lafonte.eui.eu/2024/10/10/shaping-future-generations-analysing-teacher-recruitment-reform-in-chile/)

<details>
    <summary> Abstract </summary>
This paper analyzes the impact of a recruitment policy in Chile designed to improve the quality of new teachers by incentivizing high-achieving and restricting low-achieving high school graduates from entering the teaching profession. We document that the reform effectively improved the average test scores of new teachers. Comparing the value-added of teachers from cohorts that enrolled before and after the reform, we find that post-reform mathematics teachers are more effective, while we find no difference for Spanish teachers. Finally, we find that higher test scores account for only a small part of the effect for mathematics, which instead appears to operate through selection on unobserved teacher characteristics.
</details>

**From Free to Fee: How Emission Permit Allocation Affects Firms** <br>
with [Marie Alder](https://mariealder.github.io/) and [Eva Franzmeyer](https://franzeva.github.io/) <br>
[Draft](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5521119) <br> <i>Submitted</i>

###### Presented at: NAWM 2027 (scheduled), EWMES 2026 (scheduled), VATT Seminar 2026, WCERE 2026, TMEC 2026, Helsinki GSE Environmental Seminar 2026, NAERE 2026, IAERE 2026, Finnish Economic Association meeting 2026, SAEe 2025, Onzième Conférence Évaluation des Politiques Publiques 2025, FIT Internal Seminar 2025, EUI Internal Seminar, XXXVII SIEP Conference 2025, IAERE 2025, Departmental Seminar Utrecht University, International Conference on the Ex-post Evaluation of Emissions Trading 

<details>
    <summary> Abstract </summary>
This study provides causal evidence on firm-level effects of free emission permits in emissions trading systems (ETS). We exploit an allocation reform, causing some firms to lose a substantial share of free permits. Fewer permits reduced emissions at announcement, with effects of about 14 percent after implementation. Economic activity declined likewise but only after implementation. We develop a multi-product general equilibrium model explaining this timing through a novel mechanism linking permit allocation to firms’ product exit decisions. Firms receiving fewer free emission permits terminate their least productive products. Higher market shares for remaining products encourage earlier adoption of emission-reducing technology.
</details>
